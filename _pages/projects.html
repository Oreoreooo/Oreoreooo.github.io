More Less

Ora is an entrepreneurial project I started with two collaborators to explore a simple but ambitious idea: can an AI system gradually learn who a person is through everyday conversations?

We envisioned Ora as a long-term conversational companion rather than a session-based chatbot. Through continuous text and voice interactions, the system would accumulate information about a user’s experiences, preferences, opinions, communication patterns, and personal history. Our longer-term goal was to use this information to construct a personalized digital agent that could increasingly resemble and represent the individual.

We developed a web-based prototype integrating large language models (LLMs), speech-to-text (STT), and text-to-speech (TTS), and explored methods for reorganizing fragmented conversation histories into coherent personal narratives.

However, as the project progressed, we encountered a more fundamental challenge: storing what a person has said is not the same as computationally representing who that person is. Conversation histories may capture memories and explicit preferences, but they do not necessarily provide a representation that can generalize an individual’s opinions, decisions, reasoning patterns, or behavior to unseen situations.

This unresolved technical challenge became one of the main motivations for my current research interest in individual simulation and personalized AI agents. I am particularly interested in investigating how different computational approaches to representing individuals—such as persona-based representations, retrieval-augmented memory, learned representations, and model adaptation—capture different dimensions of an individual, and how their fidelity can be systematically evaluated.
